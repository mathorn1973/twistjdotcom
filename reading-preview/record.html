When does a contact leave a record?

An interaction can be exact and leave very little behind. To call something a record, specify where it is stored, how to read it, and how long the reading lasts.

A contact you can work out on paper

Take two sites holding x and y in the ring ℤ[j], with j = exp(2πi/5). In this two-site model the sites touch if x − y is divisible by 2 in the ring. At contact, keep their sum and rotate their difference by j².

x′+y′=x+y
x′−y′=j2(x−y)

Start with (2, 0). Its sum is 2 and its difference becomes 2j², so the new pair is (1 + j², 1 − j²). Five successive contacts restore (2, 0). Starting with (1, 0) gives a different outcome. Its odd difference leaves both sites as they were.

This is a theorem for the specified two-site rule. Contact rule and its domain.

What survives from tick 4 through tick 13?

A separate model called H3 places four ring variables at each site of a finite lattice. It lets us ask about storage. Prepare one of three inputs, then follow an archive site a and a reference site b. The reader takes the algebraic trace of their weighted difference. Here ρ = j².

R(a,b)= Tr[(ρ−ρ2)(a−b)]30

The calculation follows three preparations on each of two lattices. In all six histories the archive stays fixed at both stages of every tick from 4 through 13. The reader returns 0 for the named absence preparation, +1 for the positive input and −1 for the negative input. At tick 14 the signed records change.
