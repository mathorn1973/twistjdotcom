Abstract.

We describe projective states over the fifth cyclotomic field by an integral matrix–scalar pair. Equality and overlap require only exact field arithmetic, without a normalizing square root. For primitive coordinates, the norm of the Hermitian size is a positive integral ray height. We prove the sharp bound that this height is at least the square of the number of nonzero coordinates, and classify its equality case. In two coordinates this gives exactly twelve rays of height at most four. The same bound explains the rigidity of integral invertible similitudes. The construction specializes classical projective geometry and Schmidt height; it does not derive physical measurement frequencies.

1. Coordinates and primitive rays

Fix j=e2πi/5j=e^{2\pi i/5}, K=ℚ(j)K=\Q(j), 𝒪=ℤ[j]\OO=\Z[j] and F=ℚ(φ)F=\Q(\ph), where φ=(1+5)/2=−j2−j3\ph=(1+\sqrt5)/2=-j^2-j^3. Conjugation sends jj to j4j^4; †\dagger denotes conjugate transpose. The ring of integers 𝒪\OO has discriminant 125125, real subring ℤ[φ]\Z[\ph] and roots of unity μ10={±jr:0≤r<5}\mu_{10}=\{\pm j^r:0\leq r<5\} [1]. Each coordinate has four integer coefficients in the basis (1,j,j2,j3)(1,j,j^2,j^3), with j4=−1−j−j2−j3j^4=-1-j-j^2-j^3.

States are rays [v]∈ℙd−1(K)[v]\in\PP^{d-1}(K), d≥1d\geq1, in a fixed basis. A vector in 𝒪d\OO^d is primitive if its coordinates generate the unit ideal. Every ray has a primitive representative, unique up to a unit. Indeed, Minkowski's ideal-class bound is (4/π)2(4!/44)125<2(4/\pi)^2(4!/4^4)\sqrt{125}<2; every class therefore contains an integral ideal of norm one, so 𝒪\OO is a principal ideal domain. Clear denominators, then divide by a generator of the coordinate ideal. If w=avw=av and both are primitive, 𝒪=a𝒪\OO=a\OO forces a∈𝒪×a\in\OO^\times.

2. An integral pair for a ray

For nonzero v,w∈𝒪dv,w\in\OO^d, set h(v,w)=v†wh(v,w)=v^\dagger w and H(v)=h(v,v)H(v)=h(v,v). The two embeddings σ1(j)=j\sigma_1(j)=j and σ2(j)=j2\sigma_2(j)=j^2 represent the conjugate pairs of embeddings of KK. Their restrictions to FF are denoted τ1,τ2\tau_1,\tau_2. Then H(v)∈ℤ[φ]H(v)\in\Z[\ph] and τr(H(v))=∑i|σr(vi)|2>0\tau_r(H(v))=\sum_i|\sigma_r(v_i)|^2>0 for r=1,2r=1,2. Store the homogeneous pair

(Rv,sv)=(vv†,H(v)),ρv=Rv/sv∈Md(K).(R_v,s_v)=(vv^\dagger,H(v)),\qquad \rho_v=R_v/s_v\in M_d(K).
(1)

This is the familiar rank-one projector representation of projective geometry [2], with numerator and denominator kept integral.

Proposition 1.

The matrix ρv\rho_v is Hermitian, idempotent and of trace one. It determines the ray, and

[v]=[w]⇔swRv=svRw.[v]=[w]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s_wR_v=s_vR_w.
(2)

The projective overlap is

P([v],[w])=h(v,w)h(v,w)¯H(v)H(w)=tr(ρvρw)∈F.P([v],[w])=\frac{h(v,w)\overline{h(v,w)}}{H(v)H(w)} =\tr(\rho_v\rho_w)\in F.
(3)

It is unchanged by nonzero scalars in KK, and 0≤τr(P)≤10\leq\tau_r(P)\leq1 for both embeddings. Moreover P=0P=0 precisely when h(v,w)=0h(v,w)=0, and P=1P=1 precisely when the rays coincide.

Proof.

Use Rv2=H(v)RvR_v^2=H(v)R_v and trRv=H(v)\tr R_v=H(v). The image of ρv\rho_v is KvKv. Scaling by a∈K×a\in K^\times multiplies both entries by aa¯a\bar a; cross multiplication gives (2). Multiplying the two rank-one matrices gives (3). Its numerator is fixed by conjugation. Cauchy–Schwarz gives both bounds. Zero overlap means zero inner product; equality at one in an embedding means proportional complex vectors; choosing a nonzero coordinate makes the proportionality factor an element of KK.

□

For example, v=(1,1)v=(1,1) and w=(1,j)w=(1,j) give

P([v],[w])=1+φ4,τ1(P)=3+58,τ2(P)=3−58.P([v],[w])=\frac{1+\ph}{4},\qquad \tau_1(P)=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{8},\qquad \tau_2(P)=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{8}.
(4)

One exact element has two real readings.

3. A sharp height bound

For a primitive representative write H(v)=a+bφH(v)=a+b\ph and define

Δ([v])=NF/ℚ(H(v))=a2+ab−b2.\Delta([v])=\Nm_{F/\Q}(H(v))=a^2+ab-b^2.
(5)

This positive integer is unchanged by units: replacing vv by uvuv multiplies it by NK/ℚ(u)=1\Nm_{K/\Q}(u)=1, a positive integer unit. Primitivity matters: for example (2,0)(2,0) represents [1:0][1:0] but its unreduced norm is 1616, whereas the ray height is 11.

This is the relative Schmidt height of the line KvKv, not a new height construction. In the product over all four embeddings, the two Euclidean lengths of each conjugate pair multiply to τr(H(v))\tau_r(H(v)); the coordinate-ideal norm is one for primitive vv. Thus the definition in [3], equation (2.1), gives exactly Δ\Delta. Its absolute Euclidean normalization is H2([v])=Δ([v])1/4H_2([v])=\Delta([v])^{1/4}.

Proposition 2.

If a primitive vv has kk nonzero coordinates, then

Δ([v])≥k2.\Delta([v])\geq k^2.
(6)

Equality holds precisely when all its nonzero coordinates are uεiu\varepsilon_i, for one unit u∈𝒪×u\in\OO^\times and εi∈μ10\varepsilon_i\in\mu_{10}.

Proof.

For nonzero coordinates put xi=|σ1(vi)|x_i=|\sigma_1(v_i)| and yi=|σ2(vi)|y_i=|\sigma_2(v_i)|. The norm of every nonzero algebraic integer in KK is a positive integer. Hence

Δ=(∑ixi2)(∑iyi2)≥(∑ixiyi)2=(∑iNK/ℚ(vi))2≥k2.\Delta=(\sum_i x_i^2)(\sum_i y_i^2) \geq(\sum_i x_i y_i)^2 =\left(\sum_i\sqrt{\Nm_{K/\Q}(v_i)}\right)^2\geq k^2.
(7)

Equality forces each coordinate norm to be one, so each coordinate is a unit, and Cauchy–Schwarz forces all xi/yix_i/y_i to agree. Since xiyi=1x_i y_i=1, the xix_i agree and the yiy_i agree. The ratio of any two nonzero coordinates is then an algebraic integer with all conjugates of modulus one, hence a root of unity. For completeness, this last assertion is Kronecker's lemma: the monic polynomials of all positive powers have bounded integer coefficients and bounded degree, so there are finitely many such polynomials and roots. Two powers coincide, giving a root of unity. Conversely, for coordinates uεiu\varepsilon_i one has H(v)=kuu¯H(v)=k u\bar u and Δ=k2\Delta=k^2.

□

In two coordinates the complete lowest layer is therefore

{[v]∈ℙ1(K):Δ([v])≤4}={[1:0],[0:1]}∪{[1:ε]:ε∈μ10}.\{[v]\in\PP^1(K):\Delta([v])\leq4\} =\{[1:0],[0:1]\}\ \cup\ \{[1:\varepsilon]:\varepsilon\in\mu_{10}\}.
(8)

Primitivity gives height one on the two axes; the equality case gives the ten distinct ratios at height four. Heights two and three do not occur.

4. Which integral transformations can mix rays?

For A∈GLd(K)A\in GL_d(K), clearing a common denominator gives an integral representative of its projective action. The pair updates by R↦ARA†R\mapsto ARA^\dagger, s↦tr(ARA†)s\mapsto\tr(ARA^\dagger). Before computing the new ray height, make the vector primitive.

Proposition 3.

If A∈GLd(𝒪)A\in GL_d(\OO) and A†A=cIA^\dagger A=cI, then A=uDΠA=uD\Pi, where u∈𝒪×u\in\OO^\times, DD is diagonal with entries in μ10\mu_{10}, and Π\Pi is a permutation matrix. For c=1c=1, all nonzero entries lie in μ10\mu_{10}.

Proof.

Each column has Hermitian size cc, so c∈ℤ[φ]c\in\Z[\ph] is totally positive. Taking determinants and then norms gives NF/ℚ(c)d=NK/ℚ(detA)=1\Nm_{F/\Q}(c)^d=\Nm_{K/\Q}(\det A)=1. Every column is primitive, since the corresponding row of A−1A^{-1} gives a linear combination of its entries equal to one. Its height is therefore one; Proposition 2 leaves exactly one nonzero entry, a unit. Invertibility places these entries in different rows. They have the same Hermitian size cc, so their ratios have all conjugates of modulus one and lie in μ10\mu_{10}. If c=1c=1, the entries themselves satisfy Kronecker's lemma.

□

Thus an invertible integral standard similitude cannot mix coordinates. An integral matrix invertible only over KK can:

A=(111−1),A†A=A2=2I,detA=−2.A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad A^\dagger A=A^2=2I,\qquad \det A=-2.
(9)

It maps [1:0][1:0] to [1:1][1:1] and has inverse A/2A/2 over KK. Projectively it is an involution; no division by 2\sqrt2 is needed. The return sends (1,1)(1,1) to (2,0)(2,0), illustrating the required primitive reduction before evaluating height.

5. Scope

The scalar J=1+j2=jφ−1J=1+j^2=j\ph^{-1} obeys JJ¯=φ−2J\bar J=\ph^{-2}, yet [Jv]=[v][Jv]=[v]. Its action on integer coefficients differs from its trivial action on KK-rays. Height and support use the fixed standard basis. The construction covers KK-rays, not every complex ray; its weights do not derive measurement frequencies, independent trials or physical dynamics.
