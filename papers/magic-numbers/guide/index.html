Rotation and the silver step

Eight rotations bring you home. Add a silver scale, and the direction returns while the point keeps moving. Follow that difference in four exact integers.

Keep the number. Change the view.

Let m = eπi/4, so m⁴ = −1. Every element of ℤ[m] has one and only one four-integer expression:

These are coefficients of a number, not four spacetime axes. Powers wrap around with a sign: m⁴ = −1, m⁵ = −m. That rule keeps multiplication exact.

Coefficients use arbitrary-precision integers and reduction modulo x⁴ + 1. The norm is the exact determinant of multiplication by the current element. Complex positions use the proven polar formulas, avoiding cancellation when a small value has large coefficients.

Starting at (1,0,0,0), eight pure rotations give (1,0,0,0). Eight silver steps give (577,−408,0,408). In the first view, the latter has radius δ⁻⁸ ≈ 0.000866552. In the second view its radius is δ⁸ ≈ 1153.99913. Both exact norms are 1.

Smaller does not mean lost.

The silver ratio lives inside this ring: δ = 1 + √2 = 1 + m − m³. The identities below prove that the scale step has an inverse using the same integer arithmetic.

δM = mM⁻¹ = 1 − m² − m³

“Previous step” returns along the exact orbit. No rounding is needed for the coefficients. The picture is approximate; the inverse is algebraic.

The integers can become large. Reversible arithmetic does not imply a fixed amount of storage or computation per step.

One tuple. Two reciprocal scales.

An embedding replaces m by another root of its polynomial. It changes the complex picture of the same exact element. It does not change the four coefficients.

Turn +45° and contract by δ⁻¹.

Second view · each M step× 2.414…

Turn −45° and expand by δ.

The quartic norm multiplies the squared radii in both views. For M, δ⁻² · δ² = 1. Norm one describes this product, not the radius of either picture.

Check your understanding: do eight M steps close the orbit?

No, for the nonzero seed here. The phase closes in each view, but the radii become δ⁻⁸ and δ⁸. M has infinite order. Zero is the fixed exception: multiplying zero by either rule always gives zero.

A unit is not a unitary gate.

M has an inverse in ℤ[m]. That makes it an algebraic unit. Its complex modulus is not 1, so multiplication by M is not a unitary operation on that complex line.

The phase matrix diag(1,m) is a unitary matrix; it is a different object. The related number 1 + m² has quartic norm 4 and no inverse in the integral ring. Neither contrast establishes physical measurement or irreversibility.

This paper provides exact silver arithmetic alongside the golden J step. Choosing a physical update law or connecting these numbers to observations requires additional work.
